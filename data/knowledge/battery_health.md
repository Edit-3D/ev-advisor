# EV Battery Health and Longevity

## Degradation and types of aging

Lithium-ion battery degradation is the gradual loss of usable energy storage and power capability. Calendar aging occurs with time even when a vehicle is parked. Cycle aging is associated with charging, discharging, and total energy throughput. Both are influenced by cell chemistry, pack design, software, temperature, state of charge, age, and use. Reduced usable capacity can shorten range, but fleet averages should not be treated as a prediction for one car.

An equivalent full cycle represents cumulative use equal to the battery's usable capacity; several partial discharges can add up to one cycle. Battery condition does not necessarily decline in a perfectly straight line. Vehicle-specific warranty definitions and diagnostic methods vary, so owners should consult the warranty and manufacturer procedures.

## Temperature and charging stress

Heat can accelerate chemical reactions associated with long-term aging. Cold temporarily slows cell chemistry, increases internal resistance, and can reduce available power and charging performance. The battery's thermal-management system heats or cools the pack toward an appropriate operating range, using some energy. Temporary cold-weather performance loss is not automatically permanent degradation.

High-power DC charging is useful when time matters, but repeated reliance on high power can expose a battery to more heat and stress than routine lower-power charging. Geotab's fleet research associates charging power, climate, prolonged time at very high or low state of charge, and intensive use with different degradation outcomes. It also emphasizes that vehicle designs and operating patterns vary.

## Battery-management systems

A battery-management system monitors cell voltage, current, temperature, and state of charge. It controls charging and thermal systems and can reduce available power to protect the pack. Manufacturers may reserve capacity beyond the portion displayed to the driver, so an indicated zero or one hundred percent need not represent the cells' absolute chemical limits. These protections reduce stress but cannot eliminate aging.

## Practical battery care

Follow the charge-limit and storage guidance in the vehicle manual because chemistries and controls differ. For daily use, choose a target that comfortably covers the next journey rather than habitually leaving the car at an extreme state of charge for long periods. Charging higher before a trip is normal when the range is needed.

Prefer AC or moderate-power charging when the car will already be parked long enough, reserving high-power DC for useful time savings. Use scheduled departure or preconditioning while plugged in when available. In hot conditions, covered parking and active thermal management can reduce unnecessary heat exposure. These are general recommendations, not a substitute for manufacturer guidance or a battery diagnostic.
